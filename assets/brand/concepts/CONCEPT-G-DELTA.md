# The Delta (Δ) Mark

## Concept

Δ (delta) is the universal symbol for **change** and **difference** in mathematics, physics, and — most importantly for this audience — software development (`git diff`, `delta` in calculations, incremental change).

A simple triangle. Three vertices → one apex. Three inputs → one outcome. Three phases (RYG) → one convergence. The geometry IS the name.

## Why it fits Converge

- **Δ = change, difference.** Every Converge run changes something — a codebase, a report, a video.
- **Δ = the diff.** Developers see Δ and think `git diff`. Converge tasks produce diffs. Verification checks the diff.
- **Three sides = three phases.** RED (detect), YELLOW (plan), GREEN (execute). Each side is one phase. The apex is convergence.
- **Convergence is geometry.** Three lines meeting at a point IS convergence. No metaphor needed — it's literal.
- **Minimal beyond belief.** A triangle is the simplest possible polygon. It works at any size, any medium.

## Variant Sketches

### G1 — Delta with interior convergence (strongest)

A clean equilateral triangle Δ. Inside, three lines extend from each vertex toward the center, converging at a single point.

```
       △
      △ △
     △   △
    △  ●  △    ← center = convergence point
   △   △   △
  △△△△△△△△△
```

- The outer triangle is the container (the playbook)
- The interior lines are the phases (RYG)
- The center point is convergence

### G2 — Three deltas, one mark

Three smaller triangles in RYG, arranged so their apexes meet at a single point, together forming a larger triangular composition.

```
   R  Y  G
    ╲ │ ╱
     ╲│╱
      ●      ← convergence
```

### G3 — Delta + Checkmark

The right side of the delta extends downward, forming a checkmark shape. The triangle AND the check in one continuous line.

```
      △
     △ ✓
    △  ✓
   △△△△△
```

### G4 — Negative-space delta

Instead of drawing the triangle, draw what's around it. Three colored shapes (RYG) whose inner edges form a delta in negative space. The convergence point is the negative-space center.

## Reference

- **Delta Air Lines** — the classic delta/wedge logo. Converge's treatment would need to be different (not a soft widget, more geometric/technical).
- **Triforce** (Zelda) — three triangles forming a larger triangle. Three parts → one whole.
- **Git diff symbol** — the Δ in every developer's workflow.
- **Greek letter usage in STEM** — Δx, Δt, Δv. Change is universal.
- **Triangular tech logos** — Vercel, Delta Lake, Pyramid. All prove the triangle works.

## Technical Requirements

- The simplest variant is literally just a triangle. Must be distinctive in execution.
- Line weight and proportion matter enormously — a triangle can look like 100 other logos.
- The interior convergence lines are what make it uniquely Converge.
- Works perfectly as a monochrome silhouette.
- Needs to feel "tech/math" not "construction/yield sign."

## Risk

- **Delta Air Lines** is the dominant brand association. Converge would need a markedly different triangle treatment.
- Triangles are one of the most common logo shapes. Standing out requires precise execution.
- Can feel too abstract/minimal — developers might not get the Δ→diff connection without context.
- The meaning (change/difference) is less product-connected than other concepts. It's more about the name than the function.
