# The Origami Fold Mark

## Concept

Paper = playbook. Origami = the art of folding a flat sheet into something complex and beautiful.

This is the most conceptually rich direction. The playbook is a flat document (`.converge/playbooks/{name}/`). Run it, and it "folds" into a working system — a tree of tasks, a dependency graph, a verified output. The transformation is the magic.

Fold lines create the convergence geometry naturally: creases all meet at points. The RYG colors can run along the crease lines, converging where multiple folds meet.

## Why it fits Converge

- **Playbook = paper.** The playbook is literally files on disk.
- **Flat → dimensional.** A playbook is static; a run is dynamic. Folding is the transformation.
- **Precision.** Origami requires exact folds. Verification requires exact checks.
- **Emergence.** A flat square becomes a bird, a flower, a dragon. A playbook becomes a fullstack app, a research paper, a film.
- **"Impossible from flat."** What emerges from a playbook would be impossible to hand-code in the same time.

## Variant Sketches

### F2 — Geometric Fold Pattern (strongest)

A regular hexagon with three fold lines converging from alternate vertices to the center. Creates a 3D cube illusion.

```
     ╱╲
    ╱  ╲
   ╱    ╲
  ╱   ●  ╲     ← center = convergence point
  ╲   ╱  ╱
   ╲ ╱  ╱
    ╲  ╱
     ╲╱
```

- Three fold lines (RYG) converge at center
- The hexagon becomes a 3D cube reading
- The center = convergence point (purple)

### F1 — Abstract Fold

A simpler version: a square with a single diagonal fold line, one corner folded over, revealing the "inside" color. The folded corner forms a triangle — a delta — at the convergence point.

### Variations

- The fold could create a subtle "C" shape (for Converge)
- The crease pattern could form a checkmark in the negative space
- Multiple folds could create a geometric flower/mandala (RYG cycling)

## Reference

- **Mitsubishi** — three diamonds, geometric, origami feel
- **Origami crane** — the universal symbol of origami. Can be abstracted to geometry.
- **Hexagonal fold patterns** — common in technical/geometric logos
- **Papercraft / paper engineering** — the art of pop-up books and folded structures
- **Isometric cube** — Notion's logo uses this. The fold pattern creates a similar 3D-from-2D feel.

## Technical Requirements

- The simplest variant should be just 3-4 lines (the fold creases)
- Must read as "folded paper" without being too literal
- The convergence point must be prominent
- Works in monochrome by using line weight instead of color

## Risk

- Origami can feel like an arts-and-crafts brand, not a developer tool. Execution matters.
- The hexagon/cube reading can feel like Notion's territory. Need differentiation.
