# User Personas — Portfolio Page

## Persona 1: The Screening Client

**Name**: Rebecca
**Age**: 34
**Role**: Marketing Director at a mid-size professional services firm

### Demographics
- Works in a corporate environment (80-person firm)
- Has budget authority for creative projects ($5K–$50K range)
- Browses portfolios on desktop during work hours, on mobile during commute
- Makes initial recommendations to her team; they often review together

### Goals
- Quickly assess whether a designer's style fits her firm's brand
- Find contact information without friction or hunting
- Form a quality impression in under 60 seconds
- Get a sense of the designer's experience level and specialization

### Pain Points
- Portfolio sites that load slowly or have poor image quality feel unprofessional — she assumes the work will be too
- Navigation confusion on multi-page portfolios wastes time; she abandons sites that require exploration
- Cannot tell if work is recent or current — dating or timeline would help
- Multi-page portfolios make it hard to get a holistic view before moving on

### Behaviors
- Google-searches the designer's name and checks multiple platforms
- Shares links directly with team members for second opinions
- Will visit the site on both desktop and mobile before deciding to reach out
- Reads "About" sections to judge fit — personality matters as much as work

### Tech Proficiency
- Medium — uses web daily for work, comfortable navigating complex sites
- Not a power user; won't edit CSS or dig into code
- Expects standard web conventions to work as expected

### Quote
> *"I probably spend 30 seconds max on a portfolio before deciding to reach out or move on. If it feels slow or looks dated, I assume the work will be too."*

## Persona 2: The Returning Inquirer

**Name**: Marcus
**Age**: 41
**Role**: Founder of a small e-commerce brand (12 employees)

### Demographics
- Small business owner with direct budget authority
- Has already browsed the portfolio once; returning with intent to contact
- Uses email as primary business communication
- Browses on mobile (iPhone) more than desktop

### Goals
- Find clear contact information quickly — already decided to reach out
- Understand pricing / availability without filling out a form
- Get a sense of the designer's process and personality before emailing
- Confirm the designer is actively taking new work

### Pain Points
- Contact forms feel impersonal — he prefers direct email
- Cannot find whether the designer is available without asking
- Multi-page sites with no clear "About" section make it hard to trust
- Forms ask for project details before he's ready to share them

### Behaviors
- Returns to the site via a bookmark or email thread
- Will email directly if an address is visible; avoids forms
- Looks for "About" or "Process" sections to feel confident before reaching out
- Checks LinkedIn from the portfolio to verify identity

### Tech Proficiency
- Low — comfortable with email, avoids forms and complex interfaces
- Can navigate websites but not comfortable with code or web tools
- Expects contact information to be visible and human-readable

### Quote
> *"I don't want to fill out a contact form like I'm applying for a job. If I can just email them directly, I'll reach out. If I can't find their email, I'll move on to the next designer."*
