# Taste Calibration for Premium Developer-Tool Names

## Reference lessons

- **Vite** — Strong: four letters, bright vowel, means fast in French, excellent CLI feel. Risk: pronunciation may vary, but brevity wins.
- **Linear** — Strong: a familiar precision word that implies order, direction, and low-friction work. Weakness: broad dictionary term, but premium execution makes it ownable.
- **Stripe** — Strong: concrete noun with speed, lines, payments rails, and clean visual identity. Risk: not inherently technical without context.
- **Prisma** — Strong: altered real word with light, color, and structure associations; smooth and memorable. Risk: slightly abstract.
- **Temporal** — Strong: exact technical primitive for time/workflows; serious and durable. Risk: longer, more enterprise than playful.
- **Dagster** — Strong: developer-native reference to DAGs plus friendly suffix. Risk: suffix can feel mascot-like.
- **Vercel** — Strong: coined but crisp; evokes vertex, velocity, and cells. Risk: needs brand exposure to explain itself.
- **Svelte** — Strong: real word meaning lean/elegant; perfectly matches product promise. Risk: pronunciation/spelling unfamiliar to some.
- **Bun** — Strong: tiny, concrete, warm, one syllable, excellent command name. Risk: can feel too casual unless product is excellent.
- **Raycast** — Strong: technical graphics term with projection, precision, and motion; two clear syllables. Risk: some domain specificity.
- **Tailscale** — Strong: technical/network metaphor with approachable animal anatomy plus scale. Risk: slightly longer, whimsical.
- **Fly** — Strong: absolute brevity, speed, deployment metaphor, perfect shell command. Risk: very broad and hard to search/protect.
- **Supabase** — Strong: understandable relation to Firebase, communicates database/platform. Risk: derivative and includes generic “base.”
- **Turborepo** — Strong: speed plus repository utility; obvious developer promise. Risk: descriptive and tied to one category.
- **Playwright** — Strong: craft metaphor for scripts and browser automation; memorable and dignified. Risk: longer and less CLI-snappy.

## Phonetic patterns

Premium dev-tool names tend to be short: usually 3–9 letters, 1–3 syllables, and easy to say in a standup or type in a terminal. They prefer clean consonant frames: V-T, L-N-R, S-TR-P, B-N, F-L-Y. Hard stops and fricatives add precision; open vowels add speed and lightness. The best names survive being used as commands: `vite`, `bun`, `fly`, `stripe`, `linear`.

## Semantic patterns

The strongest names borrow meaning from real objects, primitives, craft, motion, proof, speed, clarity, or structure. They do not announce “AI agent platform”; they imply competence. Good names often have a literal layer and a product-metaphor layer: Stripe = rails/lines/payment flow; Raycast = projection/precision; Svelte = lean output; Temporal = time as infrastructure.

## Premium vs. random SaaS coinage

Premium names feel inevitable after hearing the product promise. They are pronounceable, visually balanced, restrained, and confident. Random SaaS coinage leans on noisy morphemes, trend terms, unexplained suffixes, or generic positivity. A premium name can be abstract, but it should have a crisp sound, a plausible metaphor, and no need for a paragraph of defense.

## Reject examples

Reject names like AgentFlow, LangPilot, ChainCrew, AICopilotX, DevGenius, PromptOpsly, Taskifyr, AutomagicAI, NeuralForgeHub, HyperSyncly, CrewMind, and StackWizard. They overfit trends, include generic AI/category labels, sound like commodity SaaS, are awkward to pronounce, or require too much explanation.
